01 · About

What this proposal covers

Payeconnect designs practical systems for organizations that need reliable day-to-day operations — not bloated platforms. This proposal is prepared for Lawrence and Society For Less Fortunate People Project (SLFPP).

It is written in two clear parts: what is included now — the complete loan management system you can already click through in the sample — and what comes next, the items you also asked for: Accounting, Billables and messages to clients on their phones (WhatsApp / SMS reminders and collections). 02 · Context

Built around how SLFPP actually works

People walk into the program from communities across Monrovia — Red Light, Paynesville, Sinkor, West Point and beyond. Field officers verify them. Loans support livelihoods: market tables, cook shops, tailoring machines, freezers, farm inputs and emergencies. Some repay weekly on market day, some monthly, some every two weeks around the harvest. Staff need one credible system — not notebooks and chat threads.

“Korto Mulbah missed Tuesday, Mary Kollie pays today, and Daniel’s freezer loan is approved and ready to disburse” should be on screen before anyone has to remember it.

05 · Loan lifecycle

From application to final payment — every step recorded

  1. Create — a field officer picks the borrower and product, enters item value (e.g. a sewing machine at LRD 140,000) and initial deposit (LRD 20,000). The system shows principal financed, interest, fees, total repayable and the installment. Status: Processing.
  2. Review & approve — Admin or Branch Manager checks documents, income vs installment and other loans, then approves. The full schedule is generated automatically. Status: Approved.
  3. Disburse — cash, mobile money, bank or direct supplier payment for items. The loan becomes Active and the first installment appears in Due Payments and Reminders.
  4. Collect — staff record repayments against one or many installments, including paying ahead, with receipt, reference, collector and note. Late penalties apply per product rules.
  5. Close — when the last installment is paid the loan closes automatically and stays in the borrower’s track record.
06 · Daily routine

How staff run collections each morning

  1. Open Due Payments — start with Overdue (e.g. Korto Mulbah, LN-1095, 6 days late with LRD 500 penalty), then Due today.
  2. Work the reminder queue — contact each borrower on their preferred channel (WhatsApp, SMS or call) and mark them queued.
  3. Record repayments — select installments (or pay ahead), method, mobile money reference, receipt.
  4. Check this week — plan the 3-day and weekly lists so market-day collections are not rushed.
  5. Supervisor review — dashboard shows collected today, repaid % by branch and loans waiting for approval.

09 · How to review

A ten-minute walk through the sample

  1. Sign in as Admin — look at the Dashboard, then open Branches, Loan Products and Settings & Roles (try creating a custom role).
  2. Register a borrower — Incoming Registration, open the full profile section, then Verify until they are Eligible.
  3. Create a loan with a deposit — Loans → New loan; watch the installment calculate. It starts in Processing.
  4. Approve & disburse — the schedule appears and the loan becomes Active.
  5. Collect — Due Payments buckets, then Repayments: pay two installments at once and print the receipt. Check the Dashboard percentages move.
  6. Switch role — sign in as Branch Manager and Field Officer to see what each can do; finish in Reports (print / CSV) and the “Coming next” previews.

Sample data is illustrative only — not live SLFPP records. Uploads stay in your browser.
